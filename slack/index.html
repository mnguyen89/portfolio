More teams were joining Slack. Fewer were sticking around.

Team creation hit record levels while activation declined three years running. Our answer had been to keep improving Day 1 — simpler signup, better explanation, faster setup. Research and experiments kept pointing somewhere else.

  • −9% New Returned Work Teams at D14, YoY
  • 58% of team creators did not return after Day 1
  • 01

    No clear path to value

    New workspaces felt overwhelming — competing prompts, no obvious happy path, and little sense of progress. Most teams dropped off before inviting anyone.

  • 02

    No sense of who had arrived

    Every creator got the same onboarding. A temporary project workspace and a team moving off Microsoft Teams were treated alike.

  • 03

    A trial on a separate track

    For teams with real intent to buy, the first week never made the case for a paid plan. Onboarding and the trial were built by different teams, for different goals.

One journey, owned across two teams

Instead of front-loading education, setup and monetization, we could spread them across the trial. I led experience strategy and creative direction across three squads, pulling signup, onboarding, setup and activation onto a single timeline.

It also meant working outside Activate. I partnered with design on the Convert side to bring both strategies into one deliverable for product and design leadership — connecting how teams build value with when Slack asks them to pay for it.

Four connected pieces

Each one reads from what we learned about the team, so every moment is shaped by the one before it.

  • Adaptive signup

    Two segmentation questions during team creation, with one rule: if the answer doesn’t change the experience, don’t ask it. Those signals became the foundation for everything downstream, and the questions are now live for all eligible new teams.

  • New Team Launchpad

    A persistent, role-aware set of actions for the first seven days — collaboration first, tailored to what the team told us, rather than a generic checklist. Creators and joiners each get their own next step.

  • Slackbot onboarding

    Rather than concentrating education in the first session, we repositioned Slackbot as a guide that keeps going, with prompts shaped by team context.

    A scripted product-tour variant hurt Day-14 retention and we didn’t ship it. The conversational landing is what carried forward.

  • Best of Slack trial

    Looking across the full trial exposed the disconnect: we were asking for the purchase before a team had anything to use it on. Working with Convert, we reshaped the trial to build value first. Build sticky activation and conversion follows → better than persuading someone not to cancel after they’ve bought.
